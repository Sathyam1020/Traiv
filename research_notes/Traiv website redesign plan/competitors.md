# Competitor landing page research

## What do Coachway and EliteTrainer do well?

### Takeaway
Both make the actual product carry the explanation. Traiv should borrow their evidence-first sequence and focused screen crops, with original Traiv assets and verified features.

### Cited Findings
- Coachway opens with its coaching proposition, trial/demo calls to action, product imagery, and coach proof. Later it explicitly presents a chaptered product recording covering check-ins, progress, nutrition and workout building. Its client-app section exposes food, workouts, progress and chat, followed by a populated check-in example with ratings, responses and weight figures. Feature narratives sit beside tangible interface content. — [Coachway homepage](https://coachway.io/)
- EliteTrainer leads with time and client-experience benefits, paired trial/demo actions, then dashboard/training/nutrition/habits/client screen choices. It continues into training, nutrition, progress, communication and client-app explanations supported by screenshots. Its training examples show reuse, exercise media and configurable programs. The later page includes customer video testimony, tiered pricing, an ROI calculator and FAQs. Its own page currently has conflicting exercise-library totals (850+ in a feature block versus 160 in FAQ), illustrating why Traiv should maintain a single source of truth for claims. — [EliteTrainer homepage](https://elitetrainer.fit/)

### Inferences
- Put a convincing product view immediately after the hero. Explain one real coaching job at a time; avoid six paragraphs before the first useful interface image.
- Show both sides of a workflow: coach assigns a workout → client sees sessions and logs sets; client submits check-in → coach sees measurements and photos. This is stronger than a generic grid of product icons.
- Use short headings, one brief paragraph, and two or three annotations around a large screenshot. Keep pricing important but below product understanding.
- Do not import competitor claims about time saved, adoption, exercise counts, integrations or testimonials.

### Gaps
- These observations come from fetched public page content and linked-image descriptions, not a rendered browser viewport review. Exact spacing, colors, motion behavior, mobile breakpoints and current interactive behavior were not independently visually verified.

## What do other direct competitors contribute?

### Takeaway
The useful shared pattern is clear audience positioning plus product proof, with a consistent action to try the app or book a demo.

### Cited Findings
- Trainerize groups its proposition into delivering training, engaging clients and reducing administration, each with product imagery and a feature link. It also distinguishes independent coaches, studios and larger organizations. — [Trainerize](https://www.trainerize.com/)
- FitBudd pairs custom branding with multiple themed client-app screenshots showing workouts, nutrition, exercise guides and recipes. — [FitBudd](https://www.fitbudd.com/)
- Everfit organizes navigation around coaching, engagement, management and scaling, with a prominent trial path and many product/customer assets. — [Everfit](https://everfit.io/)

### Inferences
- Lead Traiv with a precise audience (independent fitness coaches and personal-training teams), rather than every possible gym use case. Do not imply access-control, classes or complete gym operations.
- Brand customization should have an actual before/after theme visual, not only the phrase “your brand.” Keep navigation compact despite the extensive product.

### Gaps
- No exhaustive competitor census or mobile visual audit was performed; these are five highly relevant primary-site references.

## How should Traiv translate the research into a landing plan?

### Takeaway
A server-rendered visual product story can stay fast, crawlable and faithful to the app without shipping the authenticated app or complex demos to visitors.

### Cited Findings
- Primary evidence behind the proposed sequence is the public product-story structure above. — [Coachway](https://coachway.io/), [EliteTrainer](https://elitetrainer.fit/)

### Inferences
- Suggested order: compact navigation; focused headline + demo/signup CTA; trainer screen with client-phone overlap; three-step workflow; workout feature; nutrition feature; check-in/progress feature; chat/vault; dedicated client-app views; verified pricing; honest early-access/support reassurance; FAQ; final CTA.
- Recreate exact app sections with shared presentational primitives and fictional demo data, or capture sanitized real screens. Product fidelity matters more than an elaborate invented marketing dashboard.
- On mobile use cropped detail panels and readable phone views; avoid shrinking a full desktop dashboard until its labels are illegible. Avoid mandatory carousels for core content.
- Apply Traiv blue/navy and existing typography consistently. Light neutral sections with occasional navy emphasis create hierarchy without copying competitor branding.
